Presenting Complaint  
The patient presented with headache and cold symptoms for approximately one week.  

History of Presenting Complaint  
The patient reports a headache described as a dull ache throughout the entire head with severity rated 4/10, which worsens in the afternoon and demonstrates minimal radiation to the neck without photophobia or phonophobia. Cold symptoms include a non-productive cough lasting approximately one week that worsens at night, rhinorrhea (described by the patient as "cold Catalan stuff") without fever or sputum. The cold symptoms initiated due to weather exposure and have persisted for approximately one week, coinciding with the duration of the headache. The headache has been present for similar time without a clear pattern of improvement or worsening beyond the described fluctuations. The cold symptoms were triggered by weather exposure, and the patient denies any precipitants for the headache including smoking, alcohol, or recreational drug use. No fever, vomiting, syncope, seizures, or neurological deficits were present. The headache does not impair daily function beyond typical cold symptoms, and there are no red flags such as progressive neurological decline or signs of infection. The patient states this is the first time experiencing such headache and expresses concern about worsening symptoms.  

Past Medical & Surgical History  
The patient has no history of hypertension, diabetes, epilepsy, or history of seizures. The patient has no past surgical history.  

Examination  
Examination: General, vital signs, and systemic examination were not documented.  

Plan  
The patient will receive hydration, rest, and over-the-counter symptomatic treatment for cold symptoms including saline nasal irrigation and acetaminophen for headache management. No investigations are required as symptoms are consistent with a viral upper respiratory infection. No referrals are indicated. The patient will be instructed to return if symptoms worsen or persist beyond seven days, such as with fever, hemoptysis, or neurological changes. Patient counseling will emphasize hydration, rest, monitoring for worsening symptoms, and criteria for seeking further care.
